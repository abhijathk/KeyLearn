Support, and the desk behind it

Two audiences that must never see each other's screen. A person with a problem gets one form, one thread, and an answer before a person reads it where the answer is already written. You get an unlisted console that reads every message ever sent and can put a banner in front of every user in the world.

Built on what the app already has: passkeys work end to end today, Brevo already sends over SMTP, and the theme tokens below are lifted straight from keylearn-themes, not approximated.

Where everything lives

The console is unlisted — it is in no menu, no sitemap, and no link anywhere in the app. That reduces noise; it is not the security boundary. The boundary is the staff flag and a second factor, which is why every staff route says so below.

03Answering before you have to

Most of what arrives is one of about a dozen questions. As somebody types, the form looks for those and offers the answer above the Send button. It never blocks sending and never hides the button — a deflection wall that makes people fight to reach a human is worse than the volume it saves.

What gets automated, and what never does

SuggestedHow-to questions, matched on the text as it is typed.
Auto-repliedA confident match sends the article immediately, clearly marked as automatic, and the ticket stays open.
Never touchedAnything about a child, a refund, a certificate, an account that cannot be reached — and every business enquiry.

Why an auto-reply never closes a ticket

Automatic closing is how support systems teach people that writing in is pointless. Here the automatic answer is the first reply, not the last one, and the ticket still shows in your inbox as needing a human until you say otherwise. If the article solved it, they tap "this solved it" and it closes itself.

The same text, three places

One library. The form suggests from it, the auto-reply sends from it, and /support/answers publishes it as ordinary linkable pages. Editing an article fixes all three, and you can paste a link to one into any conversation.

The one thing to watch

Answering from your own mail client means that reply is not in the thread — the person sees two conversations. So the forwarded mail carries a link straight to the ticket, and the sensible habit is to read on your phone and answer in the desk. Worth knowing before it bites, rather than after.

Not a public address

Your personal address is a forwarding destination held in configuration. It is never shown in the app, never in a page source, and never in an email header a stranger can read.

Rules — when an answer sends itself

move · profile · stars · streak → Moving a child to their own profile fired 41 · 84%
certificate · number · verify → Checking a certificate number fired 29 · 91%
google · sign in · no account → Signing in when your account used Google fired 18 · 61%
streak · reset · lost → Why your streak reset suggest only solved 38% — too low to send

Read the bottom of this list, not the top

"Why your streak reset" was the answer 61 times and solved it 38% of the time. That is not a bad article — it is a bad behaviour. Sixty-one people were surprised by something the app did, and the fix belongs in the app, not in a better paragraph.

So the rule for it is suggest-only. An article that does not solve things is not allowed to answer on its own, and the low score is left visible instead of being tuned away.

Matching, honestly

Keyword and phrase matching over the message text — no model, nothing sent anywhere, and it runs on the server in about a millisecond. Simple matching that you can read and correct beats a clever one you cannot explain to somebody who got the wrong answer.

Deliberately not on this page

Sign-in requirementsNo switch weakens the passkey or authenticator rule. A self-downgrade toggle is the first thing anybody who got in would reach for.

The line is simple enough to hold in your head: settings may change what the desk says and when it nags you. They may never change who can open it, where mail goes, or what proves identity. Those three move by deploy.

14The gate — abuse, and phishing in both directions

A support form is the one door in the app that accepts writing from a stranger, and a support inbox is a place a stranger can make you click something. Both directions need guarding, and the second is the one people forget.

KKeyLearn what a message passes through
1 Rate limit, by address and by network

A few messages an hour from one address, a few from one network. Enough that a family sharing a connection is never blocked; not enough to be worth automating.

 throttle
2 Confirm the address before a human sees it

A message sent while signed out waits in the holding queue until somebody follows a link in that inbox. This is the single most effective filter here: a bot posting to the endpoint never reads the mail, so it never arrives.

 hold
3 No attachments. At all.

You cannot scan your way to safety on a two-person team, and a support inbox that accepts files is a malware delivery service with a friendly form on the front. If a screenshot is genuinely needed, ask for it in the reply and look at it in the browser — never as a download.

 refused
4 Everything renders as plain text

No HTML, no markdown, no embedded images. A message is escaped and shown as characters, so nothing a sender writes can become a link, a script, a tracking pixel, or a button that looks like it belongs to the console.

 inert
5 Links are shown, never followed

A URL in a message is displayed with its full host and is not clickable. Copying it out is deliberate, which is exactly the pause that stops a tired person at the end of a shift.

 defanged
6 Length and repetition caps

A ceiling on message length, and the same body from the same address twice is folded into one ticket rather than two. Both are boring; both remove most of what gets through the first two steps.

 folded
7 Nothing in a message can act

The console never renders sender content as markup, never auto-loads a remote image, and has no control whose behaviour depends on message text. A ticket is data being displayed, not instructions being followed.

 no effect

We will never ask for your password, and no e-mail from us will ever contain a form. Open your conversation from keylearn.org rather than from this message if you would rather.

Every reply carries the ticket number and the date they opened it — a fact an impersonator does not have. SPF, DKIM and DMARC are set so a forged sender is rejected rather than filed as spam.

The rule that matters most

Support can never change how somebody signs in. Not reset a password, not remove a passkey, not disable two-step verification, not change an e-mail address. Every one of those is done by the account holder, through the account, proving they are who they say. This is the difference between a support desk and a permanent account-takeover service, and it is why the console has no button for any of them — social engineering cannot extract a capability that does not exist.

What support can do for someone locked out is send the standard recovery mail to the address already on the account. Which helps the real owner and does nothing at all for anybody else.

Rules this thing keeps

Unlisted is not a lockNo link, no menu entry, no sitemap row — that is for quiet, not safety. Every /desk route independently checks the staff flag and a second factor, so finding the URL gets you a sign-in page and nothing else.
Staff cannot be granted in the appThe flag is set from an environment list at boot. There is no button anywhere that makes somebody staff, so an account takeover cannot promote itself, and the audit question "who made them staff" has one answer: a deploy.
The console reads messages, not usersIt can see what a person wrote and the four facts they attached. It cannot see results, keystrokes, speed or other profiles — those are not in the query. Revealing a full email address is itself an audited action.
Nothing depends on receiving emailReplies go out through Brevo and the conversation lives on a token-opened page. support@ not receiving mail is a known gap and this design routes around it rather than waiting for it.
Anonymous means confirmedA message sent while signed out waits in a holding queue until somebody follows a link in their own inbox. Unconfirmed messages self-delete after seven days.
Automation answers first, never lastA confident match replies immediately and is labelled as automatic, but the ticket stays in your inbox until a person deals with it. Nothing auto-closes, and nothing about a child, a refund or a locked-out account is ever matched at all.
Business enquiries bypass everythingTagged, never automated, never queued, and forwarded to your personal address the moment they land — with a link back to the ticket so the reply goes in the thread rather than into a second conversation.
No attachments, no markup, no live linksA message is escaped and rendered as characters. Nothing a stranger writes can become a script, a tracking pixel or a button — and files are refused outright, because a two-person team cannot scan its way to safety.
Support cannot change how anybody signs inNo password reset, no passkey removal, no e-mail change, no second factor disabled. The console has no button for any of it, so social engineering has nothing to extract. The most it can do is send recovery mail to the address already on the account.
Settings cannot open doorsThe console has preferences — a signature, notifications, an away date, thresholds. It has no control that grants access, redirects mail or weakens sign-in. Those three move by deploy, so a compromised session cannot make itself permanent.
Notices expire by themselvesEvery notice carries a window. Nothing has to be remembered, and the stale "scheduled maintenance" banner from three weeks ago cannot happen.
